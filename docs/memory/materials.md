# Materials — a substance rendered for itself

Read before touching anything under `src/sketches/p5/sketches/materials/`.

## What the category is

2026-11-10 — `materials/` was opened by the maintainer when the flame, built as an iridescent sketch, turned out not to be an iridescent form: "the flame should be in its own category, Materials". Its second sketch was asked for as "a sketch based on water", with free rein. My reading, not his words: one natural substance per sketch (fire, water — smoke, ice, sand would follow), rendered for itself in a single fragment pass through `noiseFieldGpu`, realistic by default and looping exactly, where `iridescent` is one material laid on many forms. A category is only a directory; assets are keyed on the sketch name alone (`public/assets/images/templates/p5/<name>/`).

**The loop rule both sketches share**: nothing is animated on a time axis that merely happens to return. Every moving term completes a WHOLE number of cycles of the loop phase — the flame's noise lattice scrolls whole periods, each of the sea's waves turns `round( speed × growth^i )` times — so the frame at phase 1 is the frame at phase 0 by construction, whatever the form's sliders hold (they are rounded where they count cycles).

## `materials-v1-flame`: a volume, coloured by the iridescent ramp

2026-11-10 — Built as `iridescent-v6-flame` and moved the same day, before the PR merged, on the maintainer's word: a fire is not an iridescent form, and it opens `materials/`, a category of its own (my reading of the name: a substance — fire first — rendered for itself, where `iridescent` is one material on many forms; the maintainer has not spelled out what v2 is). A category is only a directory, so the move was the folder, the asset folder (`public/assets/images/templates/p5/<name>/`, keyed on the sketch name alone) and the two imports, which now reach `@/p5/sketches/iridescent/_iridescence.js` and `_form.js` across categories — legal because `_form.js` imports nothing. A flame has no surface, so the material's facing / light / structure terms have nothing to read: the sketch marches a ray through a teardrop density eroded by 3D noise (`noiseFieldGpu`, one fragment pass), reads the ramp by TEMPERATURE (`temp × (n − 1) / n` lands on the last stop of the looping ramp, never across its seam) and keeps only the `ramp` block of the form (`fold` dropped). Its custom look is a black-body ramp on a near-black ground; it borrows the iridescent ramp rather than `shader-loops`' palette helpers because the palette picker, "edit a copy" and the palette's paper all come with it, and a preset turns the same fire into its colours. Rules it taught. **A seamless fire is a PERIODIC lattice, not a looping time axis**: value noise whose cells wrap along y every 8 × 2^octave, scrolled a whole number of periods per loop; a period that long is crossed without a visible repeat, and each octave may ride whole extra periods (`shear`) so the detail does not move as one sheet. Frame 0 vs 720 differs by 1 level on 27 of 364 500 pixels. **Light is exposed on its brightest channel** (`hue × ( 1 − exp( −peak × exposure ) )`, white only past `smoothstep( 2.5, 7 )` of the exposed peak): the per-channel `1 − exp` turned every orange into pale peach. **The filaments are a flame FRONT** — `exp( −( d / width )² )` on the eroded edge field, summed along the ray so it brightens edge-on — and its width must stay near one depth slice (0.07 at 56 slices) or it dithers into stipple; without it the flame is a soft blob, with too much it is a red sack around a hollow. **Additive light is a premultiplied pixel with alpha 0**: the buffer's `( rgb, 0 )` adds to the painted background on the 2D main canvas, verified in the captures; `heat.opacity` raises the alpha so the body hides a light paper. **Mirror in the picture plane, before the twist** (`pl.x = abs( pl.x )` first), or the symmetry turns with the whirl and never shows. The march bound is 0.75 × the noise's theoretical reach (it all but never reaches its extremes), which spends the slices where the flame is. Everything is in canvas heights, so unlike the mesh sketches it frames the same at any size. 664 ms a frame at 540 × 675 under SwiftShader on the dev server. Not done: defaults tuned on software-GL stills only (never watched in motion), nothing measured on a phone; sparks are 2D and not mirrored.

## `materials-v2-water`: open sea under a low sun

2026-11-11 — The surface is afl_ext's "Very fast procedural ocean" construction, rewritten for the loop clock: a sum of waves shaped `exp( sin x − 1 )` (a sharp crest over a long trough), each DRAGGING the next ones' sample point along its own slope (`choppiness`), which bunches the small waves on the crests — a plain sum of sines reads as a pond. A ray is marched between the planes y = 0 and y = −height with the first 12 components, the normal takes all of them by finite differences and is eased back to flat with distance (`smoothing`), where a pixel covers many waves and their slopes only alias. Shading is Fresnel on the mirrored sky plus the body colour (brighter in the crests), the sun's own reflection giving the glitter path for free, and a haze that dissolves the sea into the horizon's colour. Rules it taught. **Whole cycles per wave replace the original's `time × multiplier`**: `TAU × floor( cycles + 0.5 ) × phase`, with `cycles` growing 1.07 × per component; frame 0 vs 720 differs on 730 of 364 500 pixels by at most 4 levels (thirty-two float32 phases), 50% for the next frame. **Colours are screen colours worked on as light**: linearised (`^2.2`) in JS, exposed through the ACES fit, re-encoded in the shader — picked colours added as if they were light is what made the first render a pale pink wash. **The sun's disc is `pow( cos, k )` with k derived from a half-light angle, and its apparent size grows with its power** (a disc at power 180 stays above white 2.7 × further out than its half angle): keep the power near 45 and let the glitter come from Fresnel at grazing angles. **`noiseFieldGpu` has no matrix uniform** (`setUniformOn` takes floats, vectors, ints and arrays): the camera travels as three vec3 (right, up, forward). The sketch paints every pixel, so it has no `background` field and no palette: its colours are five colour fields (sun, zenith, horizon, water). 259 ms a frame at 540 × 675 under SwiftShader. Thumbnail and previews captured in the container (`sketches.md`, the preview entry): the dev route failed silently, so the same steps were run from a Playwright script — 180 frames from the studio page in 168 s, clips checked at 180 frames / 9.000 s, thumbnail from frame 10 of that capture. Not done: never watched in motion on a real GPU, nothing measured on a phone; a mid sky colour (the linear mix of an orange horizon and a blue zenith passes through lavender, which is right for dusk and wrong for noon), clouds and foam are not there.

## `materials-v3-caustics`: a pool from above, its light computed

2026-11-11 — A tiled pool floor seen straight down through rippling water, asked for as "Caustics" from a list of v3 candidates. **The net of light is computed, not painted**: the surface is a sum of plain sines (golden-angle directions, each finer and lower than the last), so its slope and its second derivatives H come in closed form in one pass, and the light on the floor is `1 / ( | det( I + focus × H ) | + softness )` — the inverse of how much a patch of sunlight has grown or shrunk on the way down; the bright lines are where the determinant crosses zero. The usual shader caustic (an iterated sin / cos domain trick) was not taken: its time multipliers are not whole, and its net has nothing to do with the surface the floor is seen through. Rules it taught. **Normalise slope and curvature each by the ROOT of its summed powers** (`inversesqrt( Σ amp² freq² )`, `inversesqrt( Σ amp² freq⁴ )`): dividing by the plain sum, as the first build did, left the curvature at a quarter of what crosses zero and the net invisible; with the root, `focus` near 1.25 gives a net whatever the count, growth and gain. **The source patch is found by stepping back along the slope ON THE CURVATURE'S SCALE** (`carry = slope × curveNorm`, displaced by `focus × carry`), one fixed-point step, so the net sits where the refracted light really lands rather than straight under the ripple. **`gain × growth²` decides the net's grain**: above 1 the finest ripples own the curvature and the net turns to grit; 0.72 × 1.16² ≈ 0.97 keeps every scale in. **Dispersion is free**: the same H read at three focal lengths gives the colour fringes. **A glint is the CORE of a highlight** (`smoothstep( 0.45, 0.9 )` on a sharpness of 9000, the sun 50° off vertical so only slopes near twice the typical one answer): the plain `pow` drew grey smears that read as debris, then grains of rice. The floor is displaced by the slope above it before it is coloured (`refraction`), which is what makes it water and not a pattern. Colours as light, like the sea. Frame 0 vs 720: 66 of 364 500 pixels by 1 level, 83% for the next frame; 114 ms a frame at 540 × 675 under SwiftShader, the cheapest of the three. Thumbnail (frame 0) and previews captured in the container by the same script as the sea's. Not done: never watched in motion on a real GPU; no pool edge, lane line or shadow; the camera only sways and breathes, since a pan cannot loop over a floor that does not repeat.
